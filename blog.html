Op Shop of the Month

Dogwatch Variety Shop, New Brighton

Our Op Shop of the Month is Dogwatch Variety Shop in New Brighton. It's tucked into one of Christchurch's more overlooked op-shopping pockets, out where the streets start smelling like salt air, and it's exactly the kind of shop that rewards people willing to make the trip rather than sticking to the central suburbs.

Prices here are consistently reasonable across the board, whether you're after clothing, books, homewares or one of the odder miscellaneous finds that tend to turn up on the shelves. Nothing feels marked up for the sake of it, which makes Dogwatch the kind of shop you can drop into regularly rather than saving it up for a special occasion. It rewards a proper look too — the stock turns over often enough that a quiet visit one week can be followed by a genuinely good haul the next.

What really sets Dogwatch apart, though, is how much it gives back beyond the shop floor. It's recently run two huge vaccination events for the community, giving away free dog food, vaccines and wormer to local pet owners. Events like that aren't just a nice gesture — for plenty of pet owners, keeping up with vaccinations and parasite control is a real cost, and having a local shop step in and cover that gap makes a genuine difference. It says a lot about a shop when its impact on the area goes well beyond what's on the racks.

If you're already out that way, it's worth pairing a visit with a walk along New Brighton's pier or beach — it turns a quick op-shop stop into a proper afternoon out. Between the fair prices, the steady stream of fresh stock, and the community work happening alongside it, Dogwatch is always worth a visit.

Why I Love Op Shopping

For me, op shopping has always been about the hunt for a bargain. Growing up without a lot of money, buying secondhand was something I was used to. It meant I could find interesting things without having to spend a lot, and I still get that same excitement now when I find something that feels like a bargain.

These days, I don't just look at op shops for clothes. I love looking through the furniture and homeware sections because you never really know what you're going to find.

One of my favourite things I've found and worked on has been Lazy Susans. They might not sound particularly exciting, but I love finding pieces like this because they have so much potential. Something that looks old, dated or a little unloved can sometimes be transformed into something that actually fits your home and your style.

I also like that you don't necessarily need to be an expert to get started with furniture restoration. There are so many tutorials available online now that you can learn as you go — cleaning, sanding, painting, repairing and refreshing old furniture — so even if you've never restored anything before, you can start with a small project and see how you go.

That's part of what makes op shopping so appealing to me. I don't necessarily need something to be perfect when I buy it. I'm more interested in whether I can see what it could become.

For me, that's the fun of op shopping. It's not about walking into a shop knowing exactly what I'm going to buy. It's about having a look around, spotting something interesting and thinking, "I could do something with that." And when you find it for a bargain price, even better.
